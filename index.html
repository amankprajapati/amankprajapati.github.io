Publications and posters

Conference paper

Compress, encode, diagnose: PCA-enhanced quantum neural networks for breast cancer classification

IEEE RASSE 2025, Singapore  ·  Prajapati, Saha, Ahsan, Aswath Babu H

  • 96.49%accuracy, 5 qubits with PCA
  • 0.97F1, recall 0.98
  • 63% vs 18%accuracy under noise, hybrid vs classical
Read the full descriptionHide description

On near term quantum hardware the binding constraint is not the model, it is the qubit count: you can only encode as many features as you have qubits. This paper asks whether classical compression can buy quantum capacity. We reduce 30 clinical features to 5 principal components, encode each component as a rotation angle on one qubit, and let a variational circuit with ring entanglement supply the nonlinear mapping before a small classical head makes the call.

On the Wisconsin Diagnostic Breast Cancer set, the five qubit hybrid with PCA reached 96.49% accuracy with recall 0.98 and F1 0.97, slightly ahead of the 30 feature classical baseline at 95.61% and with fewer missed malignancies. The ten qubit circuit without PCA managed only 88.60%, which is the result I find most instructive: adding qubits without fixing the encoding made the model worse.

The robustness test is the other half of the story. Under additive Gaussian noise at 0.5 of each feature's standard deviation, the hybrid held 63.16% accuracy while the matched classical model collapsed to 18.42%. Circuits were simulated in PennyLane with a PyTorch head, trained end to end with parameter shift gradients.

Poster

Heat and moisture migration modeling in long-term paddy storage using in-situ sensors

Poster, ICFoST 2025, NIFTEM Thanjavur  ·  Agro-Bio Technology Lab, IIT Kharagpur, with the Food Corporation of India

  • 6.4% → 4.5%estimated paddy wastage
  • 3 depotsMiryalaguda, Nalgonda, Bargarh
  • 11 sensorsper bin, 6 hour interval
Read the full descriptionHide description

Food Corporation of India depots hold paddy in stacks of roughly 2,772 gunny bags. Spoilage does not begin in the average, it begins in a pocket: a warm moist region forms, warm air carries moisture outward, moisture settles in cooler grain nearby, and the cycle reinforces itself. A bulk average hides all of that until the loss is visible.

Eleven temperature and humidity sensors per bin report every six hours. I tessellated the sensor positions into tetrahedra with a Delaunay triangulation, converted temperature and relative humidity into grain moisture through an equilibrium isotherm, and integrated across the volume to produce a daily heat budget in megajoules and a daily water budget in kilograms, alongside interpolated field maps.

Across June 2025, the Nalgonda bin lost heat on every day of the record. Miryalaguda showed a single moisture gain of 0.359 kg on 13 June that reversed the next day, with a near zero heat change on the same day, which reads as internal redistribution or a short condensation event rather than active spoilage. The field maps show a warm region fixed near sensor S2 and a cool region near S4 that barely moved all week. Pairing those maps with zone specific drying and turning, rather than drying whole stacks on a schedule, cut estimated paddy wastage across three depots from about 6.4% to about 4.5%.

The method is deliberately honest about its own limits. A uniform half degree sensor error integrates to more energy than the daily signal itself, so the reported numbers are read as trends and signs rather than as precise quantities.

DIS(H)covering nucleus-wise gene signal counts using self-supervised contrastive learning

Under review, MICCAI 2026  ·  MIND Lab, IIT Ropar  ·  Prajapati, Mondal et al.

  • 86.41%F1 at IoU 0.5
  • 88.15%mAP at 0.5
  • 39%less manual inspection time
  • 15,500annotated nuclei
Read the full descriptionHide description

Dual in situ hybridization slides are still scored by hand. A pathologist counts HER2 and CEP17 signals nucleus by nucleus across hundreds of nuclei per case, then takes a ratio that decides treatment. The work is slow, and counts drift between readers and between sessions.

The pipeline learns what nuclei and signals look like before it sees a single label. A SimCLR encoder is pretrained on unlabeled D-DISH crops: two augmented views of the same crop are pulled together in embedding space while views of different crops are pushed apart. That encoder is then placed inside a YOLO architecture and fine tuned on the annotated subset, so the labeled data is spent on localizing nuclei and resolving individual signals rather than on learning tissue appearance from scratch.

At inference a patch is cropped from the slide, a segmentation model outlines each nucleus, and the detector finds the HER2 and CEP17 signals, each assigned to the nucleus mask that contains it. The counts appear nucleus by nucleus in an interactive web viewer where they can be checked and corrected, and the HER2 to CEP17 ratio computed from them indicates whether the patient is eligible for anti-HER2 therapy.

We also introduced the first comprehensive D-DISH dataset of this kind: 239 whole slide images from 19 breast cancer patients, of which 66 are annotated at roughly 15,500 nuclei, with public release planned. The final model reaches F1 of 86.41% at IoU 0.5, mAP of 88.15% at 0.5, precision 88.10% and recall 84.78%, and cut manual slide inspection time by about 39%.

In preparation

BLAQ: bounded learnable affine quantization

Manuscript in preparation  ·  Prajapati, Saha, Jagadish

  • 13.68 → 74.93INT4 accuracy, CIFAR-100
  • +3.78points over standard QAT
  • 0per model hyperparameter tuning
Read the full descriptionHide description

Quantization aware training normally makes every quantization parameter freely learnable. That gives the optimizer room to drift, batch normalization statistics stop matching the activations they were estimated from, and recovering accuracy turns into a per model hyperparameter search.

BLAQ starts from the post training quantization parameters and learns only a small bounded correction on top of them. The bound is the mechanism: updates cannot wander far enough to break the batch norm estimates, so training stays stable without manual tuning.

At INT4 on CIFAR-100, plain post training quantization of ResNet-18 collapses to 13.68% accuracy. BLAQ recovers 74.93%, which is 3.78 points above standard quantization aware training, and it gets there without per model hyperparameter search.

Physics informed autoencoder for satellite gyroscope anomaly detection

U R Rao Satellite Centre, ISRO  ·  Research intern, 2025

  • 0.76 → 0.84anomaly flagging F1
  • 21%fewer false alarms per day
  • 3 to 4 minearlier alerts
Read the full descriptionHide description

A dynamically tuned gyroscope produces telemetry that is noisy by nature, and limit based checks either fire constantly or miss slow drift. I built an autoencoder that embeds DTG rigid body dynamics directly in the loss, so a reconstruction is penalized for being physically impossible as well as numerically wrong. What remains in the residual is behaviour the physics cannot account for, which is a much better definition of an anomaly than a threshold on raw amplitude.

Benchmarked against LSTM, transformer, isolation forest and one class SVM baselines, it lifted anomaly flagging F1 from about 0.76 to 0.84, at 88% precision and 80% recall. Combined with cyclic redundancy checks into a hybrid pipeline, it cut false alarms from about 14 per day to about 11 and raised real alerts 3 to 4 minutes earlier than the rule based checks, which is the margin that matters for an operator on console.

Fellowship project

Explainability of tree based ensembles for medical image classification

IASc–INSA–NASI Summer Research Fellowship 2025  ·  Guide: Dr. Srikrishnan Divakaran, Krea University

  • 98.7%modality routing accuracy
  • 0.95 / 0.91AUC, pneumonia / breast
  • <0.01 sinference per image, CPU
Read the full descriptionHide description

Deep networks are accurate on medical images and difficult to interrogate. This project asked how far tree ensembles get on raw pixels when interpretability is a hard requirement rather than a nice extra. The pipeline is two stage: a parent model identifies the imaging modality across OCTMNIST, PneumoniaMNIST, RetinaMNIST and BreastMNIST, then a modality specific model makes the diagnosis. The structure mirrors how a hospital actually routes an image.

Each 28 by 28 image is flattened to a 784 dimensional vector with no convolutional features anywhere in the pipeline, so any performance is attributable to the ensembles themselves. OCT was downsampled to 10,000 training images so the parent classifier would not be dominated by it, against roughly 5,800 pneumonia, 1,400 retina and 546 breast images.

The parent random forest reaches 98.7% on modality, so very little error cascades into the second stage. Overall hierarchical accuracy is about 78%, and the loss is concentrated in five class diabetic retinopathy grading at 28 by 28 resolution. Binary tasks land at 88 to 90%, with AUC 0.95 for pneumonia and 0.91 for breast ultrasound, and inference stays under 0.01 seconds per image on CPU.

The interpretability results were the useful part. Permutation importance, reshaped back into the image grid, shows the modality classifier reading the central chest field and the horizontal retinal layers. SHAP and LIME on individual cases line up with clinical reasoning. A shallow surrogate tree fitted to the retina model exposed a shortcut: it was splitting mostly on mean image brightness, which could be genuine dark lesions or could be photography conditions. That is exactly the kind of failure a pixel level explanation is for.

Independent project

Trajectory forecasting for autonomous navigation on Indian roads

Independent project  ·  multimodal fusion of video, IMU and GPS

Driving clip with predicted trajectoryVideo link to be added
Read the full descriptionHide description

Most driving datasets assume lane markings mean something. On Indian roads they are advisory, and the useful signal is usually the behaviour of the two vehicles ahead. This model fuses front facing video, IMU time series and GPS to forecast ego trajectory, speed and turn angle over a five second horizon.

3D CNNs encode short video clips for local motion, and a sensor transformer handles temporal fusion across the three streams. Signal normalization across inputs on very different scales turned out to matter more for stable training than anything in the architecture, which is a recurring lesson in multimodal work.

Course project

Downfall: a PPO agent for a four stage 3D obstacle course

Course project, EC456 Reinforcement Learning, IIIT Dharwad  ·  with Arjit Verma

Trained agent, all four stagesWatch the run Training in progressPPO learning the course Training logsReward and loss curves

Code on GitHub  ·  Project report

Read the full descriptionHide description

Downfall is a four stage 3D obstacle course built in Godot, closer in spirit to Fall Guys than to a standard benchmark. All four stages have to be cleared in a single episode, and the agent gets no hard coded path.

The policy sees a deliberately thin observation vector: normalized raycast distances in five directions with a type code for ground, wall or hazard, a vector to the current stage goal, a one hot stage index, an on ground flag and vertical velocity. It outputs continuous movement, rotation, and a jump value thresholded into a discrete jump. Reward is shaped from progress toward the goal, a small per step time penalty so standing still is never optimal, a large bonus at each stage gate, and a penalty for falling below the safe height or being hit by a hazard.

PPO from Stable Baselines3 trains across eight parallel Godot instances at five times game speed for a million environment steps, logged to TensorBoard. All reward logic lives in the Godot scripts and is exposed to Python through a small bridge, so changing the incentive structure never touches the training code. That separation is what made iterating on the reward model faster than iterating on the policy.
